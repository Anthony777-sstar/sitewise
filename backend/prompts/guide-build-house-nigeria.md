# Guide: Building a House (Nigeria)

RULES TO FOLLOW — Building a House in Nigeria
- Verify land ownership at the State Land Registry and confirm the property has a Certificate of Occupancy.
- Obtain building plan approval from the local planning authority before starting construction.
- Hire a registered architect, structural engineer, and quantity surveyor for design and cost planning.
- Use a registered builder or contractor with verifiable past projects.
- Budget for land, survey, plan approval, materials, labor, professional fees, and government permits.
- Plan for 12–36 months depending on funding availability and material supply.

RULES TO AVOID / RED FLAGS — Building a House in Nigeria
- Don't start construction on disputed land or land without a valid Certificate of Occupancy — you risk demolition.
- Avoid unregistered "quack" contractors who cannot provide proof of past work or credentials.
- Be cautious of substandard materials — counterfeit cement and rebar are documented risks in some markets.
- Don't proceed without a formal building contract that specifies scope, timeline, and payment stages.

MATERIALS — Typical for Nigerian Residential Construction
- Structure: sandcrete block walls with reinforced concrete columns and beams is the dominant method.
- Roofing: corrugated iron or aluminum roofing sheets on timber or steel trusses are most common.
- Cement: sourced from major producers (Dangote, Lafarge/WAPCO, BUA) through local distributors.
- Steel reinforcement (rebar): verify it meets standard specifications — substandard rebar is a known quality issue.

WHERE TO SOURCE — Nigeria
- Local building material markets and registered dealers in your state.
- Buy directly from or verify authenticity with major producers' authorized distributors — counterfeit materials are a documented risk.
- A registered contractor or builder typically has established supplier relationships worth using over sourcing materials yourself if you're inexperienced.

USEFUL LINKS — Nigeria
