# Guide: Buying Land (Nigeria)

RULES TO FOLLOW — Buying Land in Nigeria
- Verify the title physically at the State Land Registry — do not rely on the seller's documents alone.
- Conduct a physical inspection of the land to check for encroachment, boundaries, and current use.
- Check for a valid Certificate of Occupancy or confirmed Governor's Consent.
- Hire an independent lawyer to conduct the search and handle the deed of assignment.
- Budget for survey fees, legal fees, registration fees, and development levy beyond the land price.

RULES TO AVOID / RED FLAGS — Buying Land in Nigeria
- Don't buy land without physically verifying against C of O and survey plan — boundary disputes are common.
- Avoid land subject to ongoing omo onile (land owner agent) disputes — these can lead to repeated demands for payment.
- Be cautious of incomplete C of O or pending government acquisition — the land may be legally unavailable.
- Don't rely on receipts alone — registration at the Land Registry is the only valid proof of ownership.
- Avoid family/communal land unless all beneficiaries have consented in writing verified by a lawyer.
