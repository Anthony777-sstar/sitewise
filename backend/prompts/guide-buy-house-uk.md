# Guide: Buying a House (UK)

RULES TO FOLLOW — Buying a House in UK
- Get an Agreement in Principle from a mortgage lender before making offers.
- Hire a solicitor or licensed conveyancer to handle the legal side — they are a required part of the process.
- Commission a survey appropriate to the property type (RICS survey levels 1–3) to uncover structural issues.
- Check the leasehold terms if the property is leasehold — ground rent, service charges, and remaining lease length matter.
- Budget for stamp duty, solicitor fees, survey costs, and moving costs on top of the purchase price.

RULES TO AVOID / RED FLAGS — Buying a House in UK
- Don't skip a survey — mortgage lenders require at least a basic valuation, but a full survey protects you.
- Be cautious of Japanese knotweed, subsidence, or cladding issues on high-rise buildings — these are expensive and can make a property unmortgageable.
- Avoid leasehold properties with onerous ground rent clauses or very short remaining lease terms.
- Don't rely solely on the estate agent's information — verify council tax bands, service charges, and property boundaries independently.
