# Guide: Building a House (UK)

RULES TO FOLLOW — Building a House in UK
- Secure land with planning permission or establish that your proposed build meets local planning policy before purchasing.
- Hire an architect to prepare planning and building regulation applications.
- Submit separate applications for planning permission and building regulations approval — both are required.
- Use a principal contractor who is registered with a competent person scheme or has relevant industry accreditation.
- Budget for architect fees, planning fees, building regs fees, and VAT on construction costs.
- Plan for 12–24 months including the planning stage.

RULES TO AVOID / RED FLAGS — Building a House in UK
- Don't assume you can build on a plot without confirmed planning permission — pre-application advice is not a guarantee.
- Be cautious of restrictive covenants on the land that may limit what you can build.
- Avoid sites with difficult access, flood zone designations, or archaeological restrictions — these add significant cost and delay.
- Don't proceed without building regulations approval — it affects your ability to sell the property later.

MATERIALS — Typical for UK Residential Construction
- Structure: brick and block masonry cavity wall construction is most traditional; timber frame is increasingly common.
- Roofing: concrete or clay tile is standard; slate in some regions or conservation areas.
- Insulation: must meet Building Regulations Part L requirements — this varies by construction type.
- Windows: must meet current thermal performance standards under Building Regulations.

WHERE TO SOURCE — UK
- Builders' merchants (Travis Perkins, Jewson, Wickes) are the standard trade supply route.
- A main contractor or builder typically handles material sourcing directly as part of a build contract.
